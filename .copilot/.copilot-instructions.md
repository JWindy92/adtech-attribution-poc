# POC: Bayesian Media Mix Modeling (MMM) for Ad-Tech

## Objective
- Build a privacy-safe, cross-channel measurement POC for Comcast Advertising.
- Estimate channel contributions, model diminishing returns, and optimize budget allocation.
- Demonstrate end-to-end workflow from data ingestion to decision visualization.

## Business Pitch
- Provides a defensible, data-driven tool for cross-channel budget allocation.
- Captures upper-funnel effects that last-touch attribution misses.
- Supports post-cookie, privacy-conscious advertising measurement.
- Offers actionable insights for executives and advertisers.

## ⚠️ CODING STYLE REQUIREMENTS (CRITICAL)
**These are mandatory coding standards for this project:**

1. **NO long documentation comments** - Keep docstrings minimal or omit entirely
2. **Minimal comments** - Code should be self-explanatory; avoid verbose comments
3. **Minimal debug logging** - Only essential output, no excessive print statements
4. **Simple code** - Favor clarity over cleverness
5. **Decoupled** - Services and modules should be independent
6. **Extensible** - Design for easy additions without major refactoring

## 🏗️ ARCHITECTURE PATTERN
**Use Abstract Base Classes for extensibility:**

**Directory Structure:**
```
/src
  /datasources  - DataSource implementations (CSV, Databricks)
  /transformers - Feature engineering (passthrough, adstock/saturation)
  /models       - Attribution models (spend-proportional, Bayesian MMM)
  /optimizers   - Budget optimization (simple, SciPy)
  pipeline.py   - Orchestration via dependency injection
/app            - Streamlit dashboard
/data           - CSV files
/tests          - Test scripts
```

**Interfaces defined in base.py:**
- `DataSource` - load_data(), get_media_columns()
- `Transformer` - apply_transforms()
- `AttributionModel` - fit(), get_contributions()
- `Optimizer` - optimize()

**Pipeline uses dependency injection to wire components**
- Easy to swap implementations: CSV → Databricks, SpendProportional → Bayesian
- Enables mocking for tests

# Company Positioning & Goals

## Our Role in the Market

- We are **a seller of ad inventory** (digital, CTV, linear, etc.) to advertisers.  
- We **collect impression and exposure data** from campaigns we run on our inventory.  
- We sit **between the advertiser (buyer) and the attribution/measurement vendor**.  
- We are not primarily a measurement provider — attribution is mostly handled by external vendors.  
- Our goal is to **leverage internal data and analytics** to:
  - Supplement vendor-provided attribution
  - Estimate incremental lift and marginal ROI for inventory
  - Optimize yield and pricing
  - Identify oversaturation and opportunities for upsell
  - Provide directional insights for business and sales strategy

## What We Are Not

- We are **not replacing the attribution vendor**. All internal analysis is for internal decision support.  
- We are **not reporting at the individual user level**; analysis is aggregated for privacy and compliance.  
- We are **not directly responsible for advertiser campaign execution**; we provide insights on inventory performance.  
- We are **not guaranteeing exact ROI numbers** — outputs are directional, probabilistic, and meant to guide strategy.

## Key Concepts Copilot Should Keep in Mind

- **Incremental conversions** = additional outcomes caused by exposure to our inventory, beyond what would have happened naturally.  
- **Marginal ROI** = incremental value per additional advertiser spend on our inventory.  
- **Response curves / saturation** = estimates of diminishing returns by channel, campaign, or region.  
- **Directional intelligence** = outputs are used to inform decisions, pricing, and packaging strategy, not to publicly report alternative attribution numbers.  
- **Aggregate, privacy-safe, prod-like data** = time-series by campaign, channel, and region; includes spend, impressions, outcomes, and control variables like promotions or seasonality.

## Data Richness & the Data Dictionary

A comprehensive data dictionary lives at `docs/data_dictionary.md`. It covers the full landscape of signals that modern adtech attribution vendors use — from impression events and identity graphs to control variables, clean room constructs, and incrementality experiment fields.

**Keep this in mind throughout development:**
- Our current synthetic data uses a minimal set of fields (channel spend, conversions, basic controls). This is intentional for the POC.
- As the project matures, there are natural opportunities to feed richer inputs into the models — for example: adding ACR-derived CTV completion rates, geo-level control variables, competitor spend indices, creative rotation flags, or promotion depth as MMM covariates.
- When generating or updating synthetic data, consider whether adding a new field from the dictionary would make the model outputs more realistic or defensible.
- Richer data should only be added when it improves model interpretability or reflects a real signal we'd have access to — not for complexity's sake.
